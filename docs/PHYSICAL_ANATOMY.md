# Physical anatomy and visible physiology

The Whole body view renders 2,277 individually identifiable meshes, 3,457 named concepts, and 2,555,534 triangles. The assembly contains the complete packaged 4.0 reference, 34 additional 4.3 meshes, five older lung lobe surfaces, and four reconstructed neck arteries. It is one adult male reference assembly, with incomplete structures and no claim of a perfect human digital twin.

## Watch a process

- **Circulation** shows the heart and vessels. The ventricular wall and associated cardiac geometry contract at the current heart rate. Blood beads travel along centerlines extracted from every source artery and vein, through their junctions, at each vessel's simulated speed: the flow of the beds it serves divided by its lumen area. Arterial beads surge with systole; venous beads move steadily. Exercise visibly speeds limb arteries and a meal the splanchnic and portal route. Pulmonary arteries use the oxygen-poor convention and pulmonary veins the oxygen-rich one.
- **Breathing** frames the lungs and trachea. Lung surfaces expand and the diaphragm descends during inspiration, then return during expiration. Air parcels reverse smoothly with the breathing cycle. Respiratory rate controls frequency and tidal volume controls excursion. The cycle readout and progress bar show the same phase used to deform the geometry.
- **Digestion** frames the abdomen. A new meal or water intake triggers a nine-second swallowing illustration. Stomach and duodenal parcels and small digestive surface waves appear when substrate is present. Portal nutrient trails appear only once carbohydrate/protein absorption is positive. Readouts connect stomach contents, intestinal contents and enzymes, portal absorption, insulin activity, and fat transfer into the model's lymph compartment. The current 3D animation does not trace the full lymphatic fat route.

The numerical simulation and anatomical animation have separate playback controls. Display cycles use current beats and breaths per real minute; 120× simulated time does not produce a 120× heartbeat animation. A paused numerical state can be previewed in motion and is labelled **State preview**. The Motion button freezes visual phase. Reduced-motion preference disables motion initially. Hardware rendering uses environment-lit materials with a 30 fps cap. Detected software GPUs use diffuse lighting, no environment prefilter, and a 12 fps cap while retaining all source geometry. In both paths, only visible triangle ranges are submitted, and a GPU fence limits the queue to one outstanding frame. Offscreen rendering is suspended. Camera navigation remains usable with motion paused.

Use layer presets, source-name search, exact structure selection, focus, isolation, hidden-part restoration, and sagittal/coronal/axial section controls. Expand opens a keyboard-contained dialog with Escape support. Section planes expose open source surfaces; they do not reconstruct internal cut faces.

## Seeing the simulation's state on the body

The body model computes far more than motion. It tracks glucose, amino acids, lipids, oxygen and carbon dioxide in 28 well-mixed compartments (blood and tissue for each organ, arterial, venous and portal blood, lymph), and the stores they feed. The body view reads those directly.

- **Colour by a substance.** *Colour by* switches from flow speed to one of the five transported substances. Blood beads and vessel walls take the colour of the concentration in the compartment that vessel carries, against resting arterial blood, on a log scale from half (blue) through resting (pale) to double (amber). Arteries carry arterial blood everywhere. A vein carries the blood of the beds it drains, mixed in proportion to their flows, so a merged vein shows the blend of its tributaries. Portal tributaries carry gut blood, and the portal trunk and its hepatic branches carry the portal compartment. Because arteries and veins share one scale, the difference between them is what each organ took up or added.
- **Organs.** Each organ is tinted by its own level against its own resting level: tissue where the model tracks it, otherwise the organ's blood (oxygen and carbon dioxide).
- **Exchange.** With a substance chosen, labels pinned to the heart, brain, liver, gut, kidneys and muscle state what the organ's tissue is taking from its blood per minute, read from the transport step's own exchange records (tissue consumption and production for the gases).
- **Stores and outputs.** Labels show stomach and intestinal contents, liver glycogen, urine rate and bladder volume, and the bladder mesh swells toward a 500 mL fill.
- **This meal.** *Colour by → This meal* follows the carbohydrate of the most recent meal only. The solver labels the meal's carbohydrate, protein and fat when it is eaten; every transfer carries the label in proportion to the labelled share of its source, and blood and tissue exchange it in both directions, so the label never changes an amount. The stomach and small intestine are coloured by the share of the meal still inside them. Blood is coloured by the meal's glucose as a multiple of resting arterial glucose (pale at none, amber at 1.5×), and organs by the meal's glucose in their tissue against that tissue's resting level. Organ labels give the grams of the meal each holds, and a strip shows where each nutrient is now: stomach, intestine, blood, tissues, stored, burned, excreted. A new meal takes over the mark. Blood compartments are well mixed, so the meal moves compartment to compartment; no front travels along a single vessel. Net-only tissue exchange in the model makes blood take up the label faster than tracer studies report.

- **Simulated time.** The stage shows the simulated clock, and the colour legend plots the chosen substance's arterial and portal level over the last simulated hour.
- **Beads stay in their vessels.** Beads are depth-tested and lifted toward the camera by just over their vessel's radius, so a vessel's own wall does not hide them but organs and bone in front do. Vessels inside an opaque organ are hidden until the organ is translucent, isolated or cut.

At rest the flows are steady, so with *Flow speed* chosen a running simulation still looks the same. What changes at rest is the clock, the bladder and the glycogen label. A meal or exercise changes the colours.

This is a display of a compartment model. Every part of one organ has the same colour because the model has one value for it; it does not say where inside the liver glucose is. The colour scale, the bladder swelling and the label positions are display choices. Hormones are not yet a colour option.

The 4.3 OBJ headers supply the exact FMA identifiers and compatibility version. The import verifies membership against the official 4.3 manifest. Both source importers retain nondegenerate source triangles and apply the existing coordinate conversion: `[x_mm / 1000, z_mm / 1000 + 0.0781112, -y_mm / 1000 - 0.1]`. Duplicate source IDs are rejected. Shared concepts union their source membership without duplicating meshes.

Curated corrections classify hepatovenous segments as liver tissue, cerebral ventricles as nervous structures, and intracardiac papillary muscles as cardiac structures. The heart selection includes FJ2428, the ventricular wall missing from the source compound selection. Archived base buffers and source IDs are preserved.

The 3.0 lung contours fill a missing surface representation in the 4.0 package. **They are approximately aligned references, not registered 4.0 parenchymal surfaces.** No warp was applied. A trachea comparison across the two versions found a 21.56 mm maximum bounding-coordinate difference; nearest-vertex distances had a 1.60 mm median and 9.67 mm 95th percentile. This is a diagnostic comparison of different source meshes and sampling, not a registration accuracy measurement. The interface identifies the older references when selected.

## Display mechanics and limits

These animations visualize model state; they do not feed force, volume, energy, or transport back into the solver. Cardiac contraction uses a smooth illustrative systolic envelope for 36% of a cycle. Inspiration occupies 40% of a display breath with cosine easing. These timing fractions, deformation amplitudes and gut wave speeds are display choices, not individually calibrated physiology. The direction of lung/diaphragm motion follows the qualitative mechanism in [OpenStax's breathing overview](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-3-the-process-of-breathing).

Airway and gut tracer curves average source vertices in 24 cross-section bins along the longest mesh axis; they are readable paths, not validated lumen centerlines. Airway and gut trails and blood beads are hidden by tissue in front of them. Cardiac and lung deformation does not currently enforce mesh contact, chamber-specific contractions, valve motion, or conservation of displayed volume. Picking uses the undeformed source surface and can differ slightly from the animated surface. Endocrine activities are numerical relative signals; the 3D view does not yet simulate hormone parcels circulating to receptors.

## Vessel graph and blood flow

`npm run build:vessels` (numpy and scipy; no Blender) writes `public/models/vessel-graph.json` from the 1,061 packaged artery and vein meshes plus four neck arteries that the source leaves out (reconstructed, then given a course registered from imaging; see below). Each surface is resampled to points 1 mm apart, swept by surface distance from both of its extreme ends, and cut into 3 mm bands; every connected ring of a band becomes one centerline node with the ring's mean radius. Meshes are joined where a vessel end touches, or sits inside, another vessel of the same circuit. Vessels that merely run side by side are left apart, and systemic arteries, systemic veins, pulmonary arteries, pulmonary veins and the portal system never join each other. Each circuit is then oriented as a tree from its heart-side root along the path of least hydraulic resistance (length over radius to the fourth power): the aortic root, the pulmonary trunk, both venae cavae and the coronary sinus, the four pulmonary veins, and the hepatic end of the portal vein. Communicating arteries are treated as anastomoses: a territory is supplied through one only when it has no other route, so the basilar artery fills from the vertebral arteries and not backwards from the carotids.

The current file has **3,665 segments**: 1,770 junctions where surfaces touch, **32 inferred bridges** across end gaps of at most 10 mm, and **17 detached roots**, all small peripheral islands. 26 meshes are skipped as copies, because the source ships some whole vessels over their own named pieces. Every remaining mesh is reached. Each segment records its mesh ID and junction kind, so inferred and detached parts stay identifiable.

### Reconstructed neck arteries

The source package has the common carotids and the intracranial internal carotids but nothing between them (a 61 mm gap on each side), and its vertebral arteries begin inside the sixth cervical vertebra, about 45 mm short of the subclavians. `scripts/vessels/build-reconstructed-vessels.py` closes those four gaps so the brain is supplied from the aorta: *Cervical part of left/right internal carotid artery* and *Prevertebral part of left/right vertebral artery* (`RECON-` IDs, `reconstructed-vessels.json`, 7,296 triangles). **These are not atlas geometry.** Each is a tube along a cubic curve between two existing source vessel ends, matching their end directions and radii and eased away from bone; the vertebral segments rise along the source vessel's own axis into its bony canal. The carotid segments clear bone by more than 9 mm. The vertebral segments are tight where they meet the source vessel inside the sixth cervical vertebra: the left clears bone by 0.6 mm and the right wall overlaps a bone vertex by 0.2 mm, which is less than the source vertebral artery's own overlap in the same canal. No imaging was used, so the course between the ends is plausible, not measured. The interface says so when one is selected, and [the provenance file](reconstructed-vessels-provenance.json) records the rules, lengths, radii and bone clearances. The external carotid arteries remain absent from the source.

### Calibre-adjusted vessels

Those four curves are now the fallback. `scripts/vessels/build-registered-vessels.py` replaces their course with one measured from imaging: the artery centerlines of a single neck CT angiogram (subject `s0504` of the [TotalSegmentator CT dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6802613), CC BY 4.0), carried onto the atlas by a thin-plate spline fitted to 19 paired landmarks (vertebral centroids C1 to T2, hyoid, thyroid and cricoid cartilages, foramen magnum, and the vertebral arteries where they pass C3 to C5). `scripts/vessels/extract-neck-scan.py` does the scan side where the imaging tools live (TotalSegmentator's open models, a least-cost trace of the vertebral arteries, VMTK centerlines) and writes a small extract, `models/neck-registration/s0504.json`, so the atlas side needs only numpy and scipy. Left out one at a time, each landmark is predicted by the others to a median of 2.71 mm (root mean square 3.54 mm, worst 8.96 mm); an affine map alone leaves 3.63 mm. Both carotid canal entries were predicted about 20 mm from where the atlas has them and were left out of the fit by rule.

Each warped centerline is then moved to end exactly on the two source vessels it joins. That correction is the honest measure of how far the registration misses at the vessels themselves: 11 to 18 mm at the carotid ends and 15 to 21 mm where the vertebral arteries leave the subclavians, spread smoothly along each segment. So the bends are real anatomy, but they are one person's arteries fitted to another person's skeleton, not a measurement of the atlas subject. Calibre does not come from the scan: it is the published adult value in the calibre ledger (common carotid below the scan's own fork, internal carotid above it, extracranial vertebral), eased to the source vessel's own radius over the last 8 mm at each join.

The source vertebral arteries' lower tips are partly sunk in the sixth cervical vertebra, so a tube ending exactly on a tip cannot clear bone. Each registered vertebral segment instead joins the source centerline a short way above the tip (1.0 mm left, 4.9 mm right) and narrows where bone leaves less room than the ledger calibre (to 1.44 mm radius left, 1.38 mm right). Clearance is the exact distance from every packaged wall vertex to the bone surface: both vertebral walls clear the vertebrae by 0.75 mm, against a required 0.5 mm, and `npm run test:vessels` recomputes this from the shipped buffers and fails below 0.3 mm. The registered segments ship as a replacement package (`registered-vessels.json`) that carries the reconstructed segments' IDs and is swapped in by ID at load, flagged `registered-from-imaging`; a segment that fails a check is left out of the package and stays reconstructed. The interface says which is which when one is selected, and [the registration provenance file](registered-vessels-provenance.json) records the scan, licence, per-landmark residuals, end corrections, radii and clearances. `scripts/vessels/render-neck-registration.py` renders the before/after comparison.

Speed is flow over lumen area, so a source vessel that is too narrow makes its blood too fast. [The calibre ledger](../models/vessel-calibre/ledger.json) holds published adult lumen diameters with their citations. `scripts/vessels/build-calibrated-vessels.py` measures each listed vessel along its own centerline and, where it differs from the cited mean by more than 25%, moves every vertex radially from that centerline by one factor. The vessel keeps its course, length, shape, mesh ID and triangles and takes the cited calibre. The archived source package is not modified: adjusted meshes are written to `calibrated-vessels.json` and swapped in by ID at load, flagged `calibre-adjusted`, and the interface says so when one is selected. [The audit file](vessel-calibre-audit.json) records the before and after.

The ledger currently has one vessel. The source hepatic portal vein measured 7.14 mm across against a pooled adult mean of 10.72 mm (Chau et al., European Journal of Radiology 2023, doi:10.1016/j.ejrad.2023.111016; abstract consulted), so it was widened by a factor of 1.50, moving no vertex more than 2.6 mm. Resting portal speed fell from about 40 cm/s to about 18 cm/s. Other thin source vessels, including the vertebral arteries and the superior mesenteric and splenic veins, are not in the ledger yet: each needs a citable mean, and vessels inside bone canals also need a clearance check before they can be widened.

With the neck arteries in place the aortic root carries the whole cardiac output. The shipped graph sends about 90% of brain flow through the internal carotids and 10% through the vertebral arteries; the vertebral share is lower than in life because the source vertebral arteries are thin and the tree gives each territory a single supply route.

Every end branch belongs to one simulated bed (heart, brain, kidneys, gut, liver, hepatic return, lungs, or the remaining peripheral beds) by its source name, and takes a share of that bed's flow in proportion to its radius cubed. A segment's share is the sum of its branches' shares, so flow is conserved at every junction by construction. In the browser, segment flow is those shares times the body state's current organ flows, and speed is flow over lumen area. At rest this gives about 17 cm/s in the ascending aorta and 10 cm/s in the inferior vena cava. Beads are placed by transit time from the root, which keeps them continuous through junctions; a 4 mm/s display floor keeps the slowest end branches moving, and reported speeds are not floored.

This is a display binding of lumped bed flows onto source geometry, not a hemodynamic solution. Radii are those of artist-refined meshes, the radius-cubed split is a rule rather than a measurement, anastomoses are reduced to a tree, and there are no capillary beds joining arteries to veins. `blender -b --python scripts/vessels/render-vessel-graph.py -- OUT_DIR` renders the centerlines inside the translucent source meshes, with inferred bridges in yellow and detached roots in magenta, for inspection.

## Verification

`npm run test:anatomy` checks additive binary/gzip integrity, finite vertices and indices, manifest bounds, concept membership, display corrections, Float32 boundary handling, cycle continuity, solver-driven exercise/meal responses, flow conservation at every vessel junction, and that exercise and a meal change vessel speeds. `npm run test:vessels` checks centerline extraction on synthetic tubes with known answers. `npm run audit:brain` audits the actual assembled atlas, including 27 new venous candidates. Browser checks exercise actual changing canvas pixels, a frozen paused frame, swallowing/absorption traces, reduced motion, source isolation, section controls, responsive expansion, and catalogue failure. These are implementation checks; biological calibration remains separate.

The browser configuration uses the full Chromium channel for both regular and headless runs, following [Playwright’s new headless-mode configuration](https://playwright.dev/docs/browsers#chromium-new-headless-mode). The final full headless run passed all 12 browser checks; 18 physiology and six anatomy checks also passed. A recorded runtime preview and verification receipt are saved in `outputs/verification/`.
